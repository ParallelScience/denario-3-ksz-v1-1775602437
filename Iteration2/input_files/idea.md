**Title: Constraining the $\tau-M$ Scaling Relation via Wiener-Filtered kSZ Extraction**

This study isolates the kSZ signal by subtracting a Wiener-filtered estimate of the primary CMB from the observed map, effectively mitigating the 57 $\mu$K foreground bottleneck before applying a velocity-weighted cross-correlation. Utilizing the 5,000-halo catalog, we will compute the estimator $\hat{\tau} = -\frac{c}{\langle v_r^2 \rangle} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (T_{obs, i} - T_{CMB, filtered, i}) \cdot v_{r,i}$, which accounts for the halo-specific line-of-sight peculiar velocities to extract the mean optical depth. By binning the halos into ten mass intervals within the $10^{13} - 10^{15} M_{\odot}$ range, we will measure the deviation of the recovered $\tau$ from the theoretical $M^{2/3}$ scaling law. The inclusion of the ground-truth CMB map for optimal foreground removal allows us to quantify the residual variance introduced by the 20 $\mu$K white noise floor. This approach isolates the baryon-mass dependence of the kSZ signal from the primary CMB field, providing a high-fidelity benchmark for the Thomson optical depth scaling in the presence of realistic instrument noise.
