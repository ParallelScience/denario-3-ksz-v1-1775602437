The observed failure to recover the theoretical $M^{2/3}$ scaling relation (recovering a slope of 0.4679 instead of 0.6667) and the significant underestimation of the mean optical depth ($\langle \hat{\tau}_{obs} \rangle \approx 1.69 \times 10^{-4}$ vs. the true $\approx 2.87 \times 10^{-3}$) are directly attributable to the dataset's beam constraint.

The dataset description specifies a Gaussian beam FWHM of 1.4 arcmin. Because the kSZ signal is modeled as a delta function at the halo position before beam convolution, the observed temperature decrement is subject to severe beam dilution. Since the physical size of the electron distribution in a halo scales with mass, the degree of beam dilution is mass-dependent. This constraint limits the analysis by introducing a systematic bias in the recovered $\tau-M$ scaling relation, as the analysis failed to account for the mass-dependent suppression of the signal caused by the 1.4 arcmin beam.
