The failure of the analysis is directly attributable to the constraints described in the dataset. Specifically, the "Physical context and key scales" section explicitly states that the kSZ signal is sub-dominant (RMS 0.14 μK) compared to the primary CMB (RMS 57 μK) and instrumental noise (RMS 20 μK). 

The dataset description defines the observed map as a linear combination of these components and provides their respective power spectra. Because the kSZ signal and the primary CMB share the same spectral signature and have overlapping power spectra in the relevant angular scales (as implied by the provided CMB power spectrum and the beam-convolved point-source nature of the kSZ), the Wiener filter—which is mathematically constrained to minimize the variance of the residual based on these power spectra—necessarily treats the kSZ signal as part of the primary CMB foreground. 

The dataset's constraint that the observer only has access to the total observed map (containing the sum of these signals) and the known power spectra makes the observed signal attenuation and subsequent noise amplification an inevitable outcome of the chosen linear filtering approach. The analysis failed because the dataset constraints render the kSZ signal indistinguishable from the CMB foreground under the assumptions of a standard Wiener filter.
