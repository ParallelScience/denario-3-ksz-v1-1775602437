We hypothesize that a Deep Learning-based "Residual-Learning" approach, specifically a U-Net architecture trained to map the observed (CMB + kSZ + Noise) map directly to the kSZ-only truth map, will outperform linear filtering methods by exploiting the non-Gaussian spatial morphology of the kSZ signal. Unlike the Wiener filter, which assumes Gaussianity and acts in the frequency domain, a U-Net can learn to distinguish the localized, point-like kSZ signatures (convolved with the 1.4 arcmin beam) from the large-scale, diffuse Gaussian primary CMB and the high-frequency white noise. 

To ensure the model learns the physical kSZ signal rather than overfitting to the CMB realization, we will implement a "Physics-Informed Loss Function": $L = \alpha ||\hat{T}_{kSZ} - T_{kSZ, truth}||^2 + \beta ||\text{CrossCorr}(\hat{T}_{kSZ}, \text{Catalog}) - \text{Target}||^2$. The second term forces the network to preserve the statistical cross-correlation properties (the pairwise kSZ signal) even if the pixel-wise reconstruction is noisy. By training on sub-patches of the 10x10 degree map, we can effectively isolate the kSZ signal and subsequently apply the velocity-weighted estimator on the network's output, bypassing the signal-attenuation issues inherent in linear foreground mitigation.
