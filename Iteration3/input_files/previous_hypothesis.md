Instead of relying on global Wiener filtering or linear velocity reconstruction, which suffer from signal attenuation and sparsity-induced failure, we propose a **Deep Learning-based "Halo-Centric" Denoising Autoencoder (DAE)**. 

The hypothesis is that a U-Net architecture, trained on small (e.g., 64x64 pixel) cutouts centered on halo positions, can learn to distinguish the specific spatial signature of a beam-convolved kSZ point source from the Gaussian random field of the CMB and white noise. By training the model to map `observed_map` cutouts to `ksz_map_truth` cutouts, the network will implicitly learn a non-linear, spatially-adaptive filter that outperforms the linear Wiener filter by exploiting the non-Gaussianity of the kSZ signal. 

To address the velocity bottleneck, we will bypass velocity reconstruction entirely by training the network to predict the *product* $\tau \cdot v_r$ directly from the map cutouts, using the known halo mass as a conditional input (feature injection). This approach transforms the problem from a noisy cross-correlation of uncertain velocities into a supervised regression task, potentially recovering the $\tau-M$ scaling relation with higher precision by leveraging the local morphological information of the clusters that linear estimators discard.
