The current analysis correctly identifies the failure of global Wiener filtering for kSZ extraction, but the conclusion that the signal is "lost" due to spatial frequency overlap is incomplete. The Wiener filter is a linear estimator designed to minimize the variance of the *CMB* reconstruction; it is not a matched filter for point sources. By design, it suppresses any feature that deviates from the assumed Gaussian CMB power spectrum, which includes the localized kSZ signal.

**Critique of the Current Approach:**
1. **Methodological Mismatch:** You treated the Wiener filter as a "foreground cleaner" for the kSZ signal. It is, in fact, a "signal remover" for anything that is not the CMB. The transfer function of -0.021 is not a technical error; it is the filter correctly identifying the kSZ signal as "non-CMB" and suppressing it.
2. **Redundant Null Tests:** The null tests (shuffling velocities) were necessary to confirm the lack of signal, but the "pairwise kSZ estimator" cross-check was poorly implemented. You applied it to the Wiener-filtered residual map, which you already knew was noise-dominated. This added no value.
3. **Missed Opportunity in Aperture Photometry:** You used a fixed aperture of 1.5x FWHM. Given the kSZ signal is beam-convolved, the optimal aperture for a point source in white noise is not a simple sum, but a matched filter (weighting by the beam profile).

**Actionable Feedback for Future Iterations:**
1. **Abandon Global Wiener Filtering:** Do not attempt to "clean" the map of the CMB globally. The CMB is a Gaussian Random Field; the kSZ signal is a collection of localized, non-Gaussian point sources. Instead, use a **Matched Filter** approach. A matched filter $M(\ell) = \frac{B(\ell)}{C_\ell^{CMB} + C_\ell^{noise}}$ is specifically designed to maximize the SNR of a known profile (the beam) while suppressing both the CMB and white noise.
2. **Direct Cross-Correlation:** Skip the map-subtraction step entirely. The pairwise kSZ estimator is mathematically robust against the primary CMB because the CMB is uncorrelated with the halo positions. The CMB acts as an additional noise term, not a bias. By cross-correlating the *raw* observed map with the halo catalog, you avoid the signal attenuation and noise amplification caused by the Wiener filter.
3. **Refine the Estimator:** Use the raw map $T_{obs}$ and compute the pairwise estimator: $\hat{\tau} = \frac{\sum (T_i - T_j) \cdot \text{sign}(r_{ij})}{\sum \text{sign}(r_{ij}) \cdot \Delta v_{ij}}$. This is the standard approach in the literature (e.g., Hand et al. 2012). It does not require CMB subtraction, only a large enough sample size to beat down the CMB variance (which is uncorrelated with the halo velocities).
4. **Focus on Scaling:** Since you have the ground truth, perform a "sensitivity analysis" on the pairwise estimator. Determine how many halos are required to reach an SNR of 3.0 using the raw map. This will provide a more meaningful benchmark for ACT DR6 than the failed Wiener-filter attempt.

**Summary:** Stop trying to subtract the CMB. The CMB is a nuisance parameter that averages to zero in cross-correlation. Focus on the pairwise estimator applied to the raw map, and use a matched filter only if you need to improve the SNR of individual stacks.
