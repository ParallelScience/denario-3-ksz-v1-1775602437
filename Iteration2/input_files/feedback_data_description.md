The failures and ambiguities in the analysis are directly attributable to constraints described in the dataset:

1. **Catalog Sparsity (Sample Size)**: The research plan's failure to reconstruct the velocity field (Pearson $r = -0.0264$) is directly caused by the dataset's limited sample size (5,000 halos over 100 square degrees). This density is insufficient for the linear theory (Kaiser formula) to resolve the large-scale gravitational potentials required for accurate velocity inference.
2. **Signal-to-Noise Ratio (Measurement Noise)**: The weak constraints on the $\tau-M$ scaling relation and the marginal detection significance ($1.56\sigma$) are constrained by the white noise level ($20 \, \mu$K RMS) and the intrinsic weakness of the kSZ signal ($0.14 \, \mu$K RMS). The dataset description explicitly notes that per-halo SNR $\ll 1$, which fundamentally limits the statistical power of the pairwise estimator.
3. **Signal Attenuation (Preprocessing/Filtering)**: The "effective signal attenuation" noted in the results is a direct consequence of the Wiener filter described in the research plan, which is necessitated by the dataset's high CMB foreground ($57 \, \mu$K RMS) relative to the kSZ signal. The beam FWHM ($1.4$ arcmin) further contributes to this signal dilution, as described in the map parameters.
