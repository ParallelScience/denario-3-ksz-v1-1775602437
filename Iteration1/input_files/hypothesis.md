The current methodology relies on a global Wiener filter and a simple velocity-weighted estimator, which, while effective at the population level, suffers from noise-induced bias in low-mass halos where the kSZ signal is weakest. I hypothesize that implementing a **Mass-Dependent Matched Filter (MDMF)**, combined with a **Bayesian Hierarchical Model (BHM)** for the $\tau-M$ scaling relation, will significantly improve the recovery of the scaling slope. 

Instead of a static filter, the MDMF will construct a filter kernel for each mass bin that accounts for the specific beam-convolved profile of the halo and the local noise power spectrum, effectively suppressing the primary CMB and white noise more aggressively for lower-mass clusters. By feeding the output of these filtered stacks into a BHM, we can treat the optical depth $\tau$ as a latent variable constrained by the known mass-dependent priors, allowing the model to "learn" the scaling relation parameters while simultaneously marginalizing over the residual noise variance. This approach moves beyond simple stacking by explicitly modeling the heteroscedastic noise properties of the dataset, which should mitigate the bias observed in the low-mass bins and bring the recovered $\tau-M$ slope closer to the theoretical 0.667.
